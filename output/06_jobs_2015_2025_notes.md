# Are women moving into the jobs where ChatGPT is used most?

Script: `scripts/06_jobs_2015_2025.py`. Tables: `output/06_jobs_2015_2025.csv`, `output/06_jobs_2015_2025_yearly.csv` and `output/06_jobs_2015_2025_groups.csv`. Figure: `output/06_jobs_2015_2025.svg`. Data: `ssb_12542_sysselsatte_yrke4_kjonn.csv` and `styrk08_openai_gender_persons.csv` in `data/raw`. Run with `python3 -I scripts/06_jobs_2015_2025.py`.

## Question

The KI-indeksen page uses employment from 2025 only. SSB's table goes back to 2015. Did women move into the jobs where ChatGPT is used most, and did they do so faster than men?

## Result

Yes. Between 2015 and 2025, the share of employed women working in the highest-use jobs grew twice as much as the share of employed men.

| Share of the employed in the fifth of jobs with the highest use | 2015 | 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Women | 16.57 % | 19.29 % | +2.72 |
| Men | 19.63 % | 20.94 % | +1.31 |

Women are still less likely than men to work in these jobs, but the difference shrank from 3.06 to 1.65 points. Women's share of the people in these jobs rose from 44.5 % to 46.2 %.

![Women and men in the highest-use jobs, and the women's share the jobs would imply](06_jobs_2015_2025.svg)

The change was gradual. The share of women in the highest-use jobs rose every year from 2015 to 2025.

## What the jobs would imply

If each job used ChatGPT as much as today, 2015 employment would give women 47.81 % of work messages. 2025 employment gives 49.18 %. The script first checks that 49.18 % is the same number analysis 01 found with the activity key's topic weights.

Over the same years, women's share of everyone employed in these 390 occupations fell slightly, from 48.71 % to 48.22 %. So the rise in the implied share comes from where women work, not from more women working.

## How this relates to the rise in messages

Analyses 02 to 05 found that women's share of messages rose by about 10 points between mid-2024 and autumn 2026. The implied share moved only 0.08 points from 2024 to 2025, from 49.10 % to 49.18 %. Changes in where women work are far too slow to explain the recent rise. The two numbers measure different things, work messages implied by jobs against all messages observed, so this is a comparison of size only.

## Method

1. Sort the 390 occupations by ChatGPT use per worker.
2. Split them into five groups from highest to lowest use, each with about a fifth of 2025 employment. An occupation goes to the group where the middle of its employment falls.
3. Keep the groups fixed and count women and men in each group, every year from 2015 to 2025.
4. **Main measure, fixed before the first run:** the share of employed women, and of employed men, in the highest-use group in 2015 and 2025.
5. **Second measure:** each occupation's use per worker times its employment, summed for women and for men.

Code 0000 (occupation unknown) has no use value, so it is left out of the main result.

## Tests of the choices

All numbers are percentage points.

| Version | Women, change | Men, change |
|---|---|---|
| Main: top fifth, groups set on 2025 employment | +2.72 | +1.31 |
| Top fifth, groups set on 2015 employment | +2.78 | +1.43 |
| Top tenth | +1.27 | +1.19 |
| Top fifth, shares of all employed, including code 0000 | +2.72 | +1.73 |

Women's share grows more than men's in every version. With the top tenth, the difference is small. The move is clearest in the wider top fifth.

The implied share with separate use for women and men goes from 47.97 % to 49.36 %. That is about the same rise.

## Did the occupation codes change?

SSB's table has the same 407 codes in every year. Total employment moves smoothly, with no sudden jumps. The only dip is 2020, when the number of employed women fell from 1,277,757 to 1,264,246. Code 0000 shrank over the period, from 58,920 to 54,795 women and from 127,806 to 103,900 men. Whether SSB reclassified individual occupations within the codes is not checked.

## Limits

1. **Use per worker is modelled, not observed.** It comes from the tasks in each job. This analysis shows where women and men work, not how they use ChatGPT.
2. **Job content is held fixed.** Today's use per worker is applied to 2015 as if the jobs had not changed.
3. **The highest-use group is very mixed.** Use per worker ranges from 1.20 to 41.48 inside it.
4. **There is no statistical uncertainty here.** The counts are full register numbers, but the use values have no stated uncertainty.

## Checks

1. Before the results, the script checks that the occupation file's women and men equal SSB's 2025 numbers for all 390 occupations, and that the implied share for 2025 reproduces 49.18 %.
2. The script runs tests on made-up data before it reads the real files. They cover the grouping, the shares and the implied share.
3. Six deliberate bugs were put into copies of the script, one at a time. The first tests stopped four. Two new tests, for the grouping rule and for separate use by gender, now stop all six.
4. The main numbers were recomputed in a separate short script with no shared functions. It gave 16.57, 19.29, 19.63, 20.94, 47.81 and 49.18.
5. A clean copy with only the script and `data/raw` gave byte-identical output. The files in `data/raw` are unchanged.
